# `s(39)` — open

**External intake, 2026-09-22.** wand125’s retained point-certificate source reports
`s(39) >= 13/2 = 6.5`. The complete exact replay verifies the retained certificate.

Open. The best known packing gives `s(39) ≤ 6.81072209`, and the verified lower bound is
`6.5`, leaving a gap of about `0.3107`.

## The verified upper bound is a ceiling

`verified_upper_bound` for this case is `7`, which is **larger** than the best known
`6.81072208306864` two fields above it.
It is not a tighter reading of the same packing and it is not the value of `s(39)`: it
is the strongest ceiling this repository can certify from its own evidence — the trivial
grid bound `⌈√39⌉ = 7` — and it trails the reported construction by `0.18927791693136`.

Its `exact_form` is the exact form of that ceiling, never of `s(39)`. `s(39)` is not
known exactly; `status` stays `open`, and the `mathematics` blocker in the frontmatter
names the gap. Read `reported_upper_bound` for the best known side length.

## The packing

Found by Thomas Schadt in 2026, via simulated annealing.
Its side length is algebraic of degree **5** over `ℚ`. Its Galois group over `ℚ` is
`S5`, computed here from the recorded minimal polynomial with `sympy.galois_group`, and
`S5` is not solvable.
**The side length is therefore not expressible in radicals**, so the empty `exact_form`
is a permanent property of this case rather than a transcription gap.

## The lower bound

The earlier record from [Friedman DS7] gives the lower-bound expression
`10*sqrt(3)/37 + 2*sqrt(2) + 113/37` for `s(39)` (approximately `6.350603018684`).
Friedman’s DS7 survey, Theorem 9, k=6, reports this bound at n=37; reference [8] is
Green’s private communication (2000). The source proof has not been recovered.
Inherited at n=39 by monotonicity.
That report remains as historical evidence; the verified point certificate gives the
stronger bound `13/2`. The [source audit](../devtools/audit_ds7_lower_bounds.py)
compares the exact theorem expressions separately from opaque table decimals.

The exact replay verifies all five covering-certificate conditions over the complete
rational direction net.
A second interval implementation or generalized native importer would add confirmation
and reuse, but it is not a missing premise of this fixed bound.

Nagamochi’s earlier general closed form remains part of the evidence history and applies
to every `N ≥ 4`:

```
s(N) ≥ min{ ⌈√N⌉,  √(N − 2⌊√N⌋ + 1) + 1 }
```
